**This page is the running record of what's new**, with the portal front and center: every release that changes what creators see, what the program pays, or how the dashboard reads, in ambassador language rather than commit messages.

**How it works:** new entries land as things ship, written in plain language with screenshots where they help, newest on top. Anything you read here is live in the product, and a quiet stretch means nothing user-facing shipped in it.

**How to read an entry:** the label is the ship date, the subtitle is the theme, and bold leads name each change. When a change alters what you should DO (a new worklist, a moved button, a new rule), the entry says so explicitly.

<Update label="September 5, 2026" description="Both staff dashboards are back after a load related outage, and the funnel's post counts now read a bounded slice of the roster">
  **The Cohort 9 dashboard at /dashboard/cohort9 loads again.** Overview, the review queue, and the post lifecycle list each pulled every program creator's full history in a single read, and with a roster around 270 creators and thousands of posts behind it, that read outgrew what the database will return at once, so the page failed to open on September 5. Those three views now page the roster in and read it in bounded batches, stepping down to smaller batches on their own when one comes back too large. The way every number is counted is unchanged, so standing, review, and pay all read exactly as they did before. The page also loads fresh when it opens, refreshes about once a minute while it stays open, and refreshes right after a review, a block, or a room change, so a decision shows up in the numbers without a manual reload.

  **The full staff dashboard at /dashboard is back as well.** The funnel counted submitted and approved posts for every roster row by reading the entire post table, roughly 31,000 posts, which crossed the same kind of limit and took the whole staff dashboard down on September 5 rather than only the funnel. Those counts now come from an index and cover just the rows in the current selection: the 1,000 most recent enrollments in whatever cohort selection is showing, and up to 300 posts per creator. **A roster row past that cap shows a dash where the posts count usually sits.** Those are the oldest enrollments in a wide selection, and a dash means the funnel did not count that row, not that the creator has no posts. Narrowing to a single cohort brings those rows back inside the window. Every other number on the dashboard is what it was.
</Update>

<Update label="September 4, 2026" description="One payment model for every portal creator, one set of numbers on the Cohort 9 dashboard, two Instagram fixes, and a signup that opens straight into setup">
  **Every creator who has opened the pilot portal is now paid on the cohort 9 per post model.** Until this shipped, a creator who came over from an earlier cohort could still be sitting on the old weekly ledger, which carries no rate and no view line, or held in a routing state that neither payout engine paid. On an active roster of 192, 83 were on the weekly lane and 21 more were held, so more than half the roster was posting into a model that could not price a post. Opening the pilot portal is now the only signal that decides this: anyone who has ever entered it is paid \$2.50 for every post that clears 350 views, with the view ladder on top, the 3 posts per platform per day cap applied, and a real dollar figure on every qualifying post. The one thing that still holds pay is a genuine identity conflict, meaning duplicate or ambiguous Cantina accounts, which is fraud and data protection rather than a payment lane. Nothing already paid was touched and no creator records were rewritten in bulk. Worth knowing for tickets: posts that read as earning with \$0 beside them should now carry what they are actually worth.

  **The Cohort 9 dashboard counts one set of posts in every panel.** There were two definitions of which posts were visible, a wider ops only one and the stricter since portal entry one, so Overview and Review could legitimately disagree about the same activity, and a dollar figure could appear next to a post the payout engine was never going to pay. There is one rule now, counted from each creator's own portal entry, and Overview, Review, the post lifecycle list, and the creator detail drawer all read from it. This replaces what the September 3 entry described: migrated creators' posts were being counted from the flat September 2 migration date, and are now counted from the same date the payout engine uses. Separately, the Review tab's tiles for Needs a decision, Still earning, and Value in the queue read 0 for migrated creators under the Migrated filter even while Overview showed their activity; those tiles now include everyone the per post model covers. Roster rows and the creator drawer also carry an active standing badge, read from the same rule the creator's own portal shows them, so the two sides cannot disagree about who is active.

  **Cohort 9 posts no longer appear on the old dashboard's Social tab.** Posts from the September 2 launch onward belong to the Cohort 9 Review tab, whose Earning, In review, and Completed stages are the 7 day accrual window. The old Social tab has no concept of that window and offered live Approve and Deny on any row, so a post still inside its hold could be closed out early from there and cut short before the window ended. That tab now excludes every post dated on or after the September 2 cutoff, including freshly discovered rows that do not have a platform timestamp yet, which were the batch still slipping through in the first pass. Posts from earlier cohorts are unchanged there. Worth knowing: review cohort 9 posts on the Cohort 9 Review tab, not the Social tab.

  **A first refresh can no longer zero out a real Instagram view count.** The September 3 fix kept the stored figure when a zero came back, but only on a post that already had views recorded, so a post's very first metrics refresh was never covered and a clip that went viral quickly could still be written down to 0. A zero is now treated as unknown whenever the same response carries real likes, comments, shares, or saves. This stays a pay question rather than a display one, since cohort 9 reads views and a post under 350 views does not count.

  **The portal's Refresh button no longer fails on some Instagram posts.** Instagram sometimes returns a post id as a very large number rather than text, and the whole refresh aborted before any post was saved, so discovery worked and the portal still showed a generic try again message. Instagram's shortcode is now used as the post id and unexpected values are normalized before saving. Worth knowing for tickets: someone who tapped Refresh on Instagram in the last few days and saw nothing appear should tap it once more.

  **Signing up now opens the live setup instead of the retired Sprint screen.** The success screen after joining still described the Sprint Room, a sign in email, and a seven day clock, none of which exist in cohort 9. Enrolling now goes straight into pilot setup. The last step of the portal tour also points at Cantina and the bot link action rather than describing it, telling creators to tap Open Cantina in the top right and then Link a bot you created on Home.

  **The setup copy now says what a returning member should link.** Creators new to Character Studio are told to create a new public TikTok or Instagram dedicated to one character, and returning Character Studio members are told they can link the character account they already use. A personal account still does not qualify either way, and linking both platforms still lets the same videos pay twice. This is the answer to the returning member question ambassadors get most: they do not need a brand new account, they need a character account.

  **The signup form now asks for email consent before it will submit.** There is an unchecked box agreeing to receive program emails, and the submit button stays disabled until it is ticked. Nothing else about joining changed.
</Update>

Worth knowing for tickets: a rename is self serve now, so that ticket can usually be answered by pointing at the Linked panel in the portal.

<Update label="September 2, 2026" description="Cohort 9 launches: one flat program, a rebuilt portal, and cohort 8 and 8R close">
  **Cohort 9 is live, and it replaces the three-stage program.** Every organic signup from September 1 lands in cohort 9 on the new v5 terms, and the apparatus the last four cohorts carried goes with it: no application to fill in, no 7 day clock, no warm-up days, no Get Set Up sprint, and no Bonus Ramp to graduate out of. There is one set of rules, and it reads the same in week one as it does in month six. Cohort 8 and 8R closed their intake windows the moment this deployed, so nobody new enters those cohorts again.

  **What it pays: \$2.50 for every post that clears 350 views, flat from the first post.** No tiers and no completion bonus, so the rate is the same on post one and post one hundred. A view ladder stacks a bonus on top of that base, paid per post at the milestone it reaches, from \$15 at 5,000 views up to \$500 at 5,000,000, and several posts can hit milestones in the same week. Up to 3 posts per platform per day count toward pay, payouts run on a rolling 7 day cycle, and a post that crosses 350 views days after it went up still pays. Nothing is claimed or submitted: the program reads view counts off the linked accounts and pays what qualified.

  **Active standing is three approved posts a week, each at 350+ views.** That is the whole membership requirement, and it is what keeps the full rate, the 5 weekly Agentic Video generations, and bounty access. It counts posts that qualify rather than how many times someone posted, and the daily post cap does not suppress it, so a day carrying four qualifying posts cannot read as inactive. Worth knowing: the standing check used to pass on one approved post in 7 days at any view count while the onboarding promised three at 350+, so the bar creators are shown and the bar being measured are now the same number.

  **What creators see: the lander is the front door, and the portal is rebuilt behind it.** The root of the site is the funnel again, with a "Join the program" form in place of an application and the whole deal on one page: the rate, the ladder, the three-a-week rule, and every hard rule that can cost a post. Signing up opens a guided setup rather than a checklist: make the character in Cantina, then link a brand new TikTok or Instagram by generating a code, pasting it into the account bio, and coming back to verify, with those three steps spelled out above the card on both platforms. One linked account is enough to finish, and the portal keeps a Link button for the platform that got skipped. Pasting the character's Cantina share link registers it, and the portal home then wears that character's own art instead of the house coin. Withdraw opens the real payout widget, the help sheet files a ticket straight into the ops inbox instead of opening a mail client, and the Community and Character Guidelines now live on a public page linked from setup, the help sheet, and the footer. Signed-in creators who go to the old portal address are sent to the new one.

  **Cohort 8 and 8R creators move into the Official Program on these terms.** Earned money stays exactly where it is and settled payout rows are never touched. What ends at the cut is future accrual on the Get Set Up, setup bonus, and Bonus Ramp lines, which would otherwise have kept running for the creators who still carry a Phase 1 anchor. The move covers creators who actually engaged, meaning Phase 1 complete or at least one verified social account. The dormant re-enrollment rows from the 8R waitlist batch, around a thousand that never linked anything, are deliberately left alone, so those records do not turn Official on their own and need a staff tag if one becomes active.

  **Two habits to change.** Cohort 9 creators belong in the Character Studio room, not the Sprint Room: the portal CTA and the intake form both point at the new invite now, and the Sprint Room closes out with cohort 8 and 8R. And post review rows on the dashboard now name the creator's registered character and link to its Cantina share page, which resolves to the account that owns it, so checking whether a video stars their own character is one click instead of a judgement call. A row with no character registered reads as an amber "not linked" rather than a blank, and that is the honest state for nearly every post today, because almost nobody has registered one yet.
</Update>

A search that came back empty therefore read as proof someone never applied, when it usually meant the program never learned their handle. The program facts behind those emails are still recorded either way, so the funnel and the roster stay accurate, and a new signup still creates its record. Staff sends now fail with a reason instead of quietly doing nothing, and dry runs still work, so an audience can be audited while the pause is on. Worth knowing for tickets: if program email has gone quiet for a creator, check whether the pause is on before treating it as a delivery problem.

The recipient list is configurable now and goes out as one send to the whole team, with the shared inbox kept first so it stays the reply-to address and replies still land somewhere shared rather than in one person's mailbox. The creator-facing forum emails, reply, thread reply, and mention, are untouched and still go to the single recipient, because each one carries that person's own sign-in link.

If you are reporting growth this week, it is now on the tab rather than a hand calculation.

<Update label="August 18, 2026" description="Stuck payouts moving again, the \$20 curriculum bonus paying itself, a safer Earnings card">
  **Payouts now send one at a time, and the queue keeps itself moving.** Our payment provider settles one transfer at a time out of the company balance, so a second send that starts while the first is still settling gets rejected by design, even when it is headed to a different creator. Approving a batch fired those sends back to back, so a batch approval could jam the whole queue: on August 17 that left 10 payouts and \$307.50 stuck for a day. Sends are now chained, one transfer per run about five seconds apart, with a longer pause after a rejection, and a job every 15 minutes picks the chain back up if it ever stops partway through the queue. A row whose transfer died on the provider's side can also be put back in line now, instead of retrying forever against a transfer that no longer exists. The stuck batch drained in about 80 seconds once this shipped. Worth knowing for tickets: an approved payout that has not sent yet is now minutes of queue rather than a stall.

  **The \$20 curriculum bonus creates its own payout row now.** Finishing the Character Curriculum has earned \$20 since the bonus launched, but the row behind it was an operator click, and then a second click to approve, and that first click had never been made for anyone: 65 creators had finished, \$1,300 was earned, and not a single bonus row had ever existed. A daily job now writes the row for everyone who has earned it, and its first run created all 65. Rows land pending and still go through the normal approval step, so nothing here moves money on its own. It pays only the unambiguous case, everyone who finished every video inside their 30 day window, and anyone whose 7 day clock never started is counted and reported for a person to decide rather than quietly paid or quietly dropped. Worth knowing for tickets: a curriculum finish from weeks ago that never showed a \$20 now has a real row waiting on approval like any other payout.

  **The Earnings card stopped showing creators our internal notes.** When a payout failed, the card printed the failure text straight from our own records, which is where the payment provider's raw error bodies and staff triage notes live, so someone waiting on \$150 could be shown internal ids and ops shorthand about their own money. That text no longer reaches the browser at all. The card now shows one of two plain lines instead: the money is safe and we are on it, or, when identity verification genuinely is unfinished, a pointer to the payout widget to finish it. Same rule the KYC blocker text already follows, translate it, never paste it.

  **A second payout wallet no longer takes the Earnings card down, and verification status refreshes on its own.** Yesterday's entry said the portal stops and asks the creator to contact support when two payout wallets sit on one record. That has changed: the provider will not merge duplicate connected accounts, so the portal now picks one, the wallet that has actually been paid before, or the most recently linked when neither has, and staff get a warning so the duplicate still gets sorted out. The Earnings card and the payout widget stay reachable either way, which matters because the widget is the one surface that can fix a stalled verification. Separately, verification status is now refreshed by an hourly sweep rather than only when somebody remembered to run it by hand, since the provider has no way to tell us the moment a status changes. The first sweep corrected 12 cached statuses, nine of them creators who were already approved and were being shown as unverified, and one that surfaced as genuinely needing something fixed. Worth knowing for tickets: "it still says I am not verified" is much less likely to be stale data now.

  **Setup completion and the 7 day clock now read the same evidence, whichever email or username someone signed up with.** The checklist and the clock resolved a creator differently, so a portal could show all four setup steps green while the clock never started and nothing on screen could explain why. Payout setup now counts a linked wallet found under any email we know for that person, which uncovered 12 creators whose wallet no path could see, and the job that starts the clock now matches a room join by Cantina username as well as by email and phone, the last identifier the portal accepts that it did not. Two staff repairs ran alongside it, linking 286 portal logins and 18 payout accounts to their creator records with no guesses. Measured after it deployed, of the 1,103 creators without a clock, zero now have all three setup facts recorded and no clock, so the stamper is no longer what holds anyone up. Worth knowing for tickets: a fully green checklist with a dead clock should be rare now, and the usual reason a clock has not started is that socials, payout, or the room join really is missing.

  **Three dashboard fixes on the worklists.** Worklist rows now carry a small arrow that opens that creator's Cantina profile in a new tab, built from their in-app username rather than the handle they typed into a form, so confirming who a row is no longer means searching for them again. The graduates list is now titled "Graduates · invite to the Character Studio room", naming the room you are actually inviting people into. And the cohort 1 close-out panel sends to cohort 1 only again: it had lost its cohort filter, leaving 64 later-cohort graduates queued for the retired cohort 1 terms, while the identity work had separately cleared the stamps that record who was already emailed, which re-armed send buttons for people mailed back in July. The panel is scoped again and the July stamps are restored, so those buttons read as sent.
</Update>

<Update label="August 17, 2026" description="Portal data follows one stable creator record, plus two new holds on the room worklists">
  **Portal progress and earnings now follow a stable creator record instead of a login email.** Verified socials, tracked posts, curriculum progress, concept tester spins, payout accounts and payout rows were each filed under whatever email address they happened to be created with. When a creator's portal login sat under a different address than their application, that split their record down the middle: setup evidence on one side, posts and earnings on the other, and a portal that could read complete on one surface and empty on another. All of that portal data now hangs off the stable applicant id created when someone applies, with email kept alongside as a contact and audit field, so both halves resolve to one person. Two staff tools shipped with it, one to move existing accounts onto their applicant id and one to repair a login that is already split. This is the durable version of the August 13 fix that backdated setup for mismatched-email accounts: that one repaired the damage case by case, and this stops the record splitting in the first place. Worth knowing for tickets: a portal that looks emptier than the dashboard is much less likely to be an identity split now, so check the posting window and the review queue first.

  **The portal stops rather than guesses when two payout wallets sit on one creator.** Pulling a record together across addresses can surface two different linked wallets on the same person. Instead of picking one and risking paying the wrong one, the earnings and payout screens now stop and say to contact support. That message means a real duplicate for staff to sort out, not a broken portal.

  **Creators with posts still in our review queue stay off the remove-from-room worklist.** When a Get Set Up window closed with in-window posts still unreviewed, those rows landed on the removal list, so our own review latency read as a failed sprint. Rows like that are now held back while any in-window post is still awaiting review, and a line above the list gives the count being held and asks for the review queue to be cleared. They resolve onto the correct list on their own once the posts are judged. Only posts inside the counting window hold a row back, since a post outside it can never change the verdict. Pay was never affected either way, because the removal checkoff does not touch what anyone earned. The habit to build: when that held count is above zero, clear the post-review queue before working the removal list.

  **Worklist rows have a Hold button now, for creators with an open ticket.** When a support ticket might still change someone's outcome, there was no way to say so on the row, so an open question could get actioned by whoever worked the list next. Every open worklist row now carries Hold, and a held row gets an amber "on hold" chip, drops out of the outstanding count, sinks below the actionable rows, and stays out of the copy-usernames list, so it cannot be swept up by a bulk paste either. Release puts it back. The hold records who set it and attaches to the creator rather than to one row, so someone sitting on two worklists is held on both. The habit to build: hold the row when the ticket opens, release it when the ticket resolves.
</Update>
